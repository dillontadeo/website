02Case study · Jamiez LLC

Where to expand in California, and on what terms

Financial Analyst, 2025–2026. Python, SQL, Excel, Tableau, and R, with Census ACS, BLS, public listings, and internal operating data.

The question

Which county should be the second market, and how should it open?

Jamiez needed a second market. Ranking counties by household income was the obvious starting point. It was also the wrong question.

I scored 50+ California counties on income, homeownership, competition, distance from San Jose, and cost, then asked something simpler: can this market cover its own overhead? Demand was not the problem. Alameda has far more high-income households than the plan needed. Of the eleven counties that made the shortlist, six could support a full market on their own. Labor was the constraint. A founder rule of three staff per ten households put every scenario underwater. Counting the hours each membership tier actually uses flipped Year 5 EBITDA from a loss to a profit — same revenue, high-six-figure swing.

The useful decision was how to enter, not only where. Open Alameda with a light footprint, add overhead after the book can carry it, and wait to open the next market until the current one is holding. You give up some Year 5 revenue to get there. San Francisco is the useful counterexample: high income, tenth in the ranking, because homeownership is low and the market is crowded. Napa cannot support a market on its own.
